Step 1 · Needs you

Connect the site’s calendar to Airbnb and Vrbo

The site already reads your Airbnb and Vrbo calendars, so booked nights are greyed out. This step is the other direction: when you record a direct booking on the site, Airbnb and Vrbo block those dates too. Paste the site’s calendar link into both, once.

Your private calendar link is on the owner page, theprosserhome.com/manage. Sign in with your email (we send you a button, no password), scroll to the bottom, and tap Copy the link.

Where to paste it

Airbnb: Calendar → Availability settings → Connect calendars → Import calendar. Paste the link and name it “The Prosser Home website”. Vrbo: Calendar → Import & export → Import calendar, same link. Both check it every few hours, so for a guest arriving within a day or two, also block the dates by hand.

The link only shows which nights are booked directly, with no names or guest details. It stays on the owner page so it’s never on a shared page like this one.

Built

What happens now

  • A guest asks. You both get the email and the text as before — but now with one button: Open this request. No password, no app.
  • That page does your homework. While you’re looking at it, it checks Airbnb, Vrbo and the website calendar for a clash, and works out the all-in price for those nights.
  • One tap on Accept does all four things. It takes the dates off the calendar, sends the guest the price and your Venmo link, tells them the dates are held for them and until when, and texts you and Ben what went out.
  • You can’t both reply. Whoever taps first handles it. The other one opens the link and sees “accepted by Tyler, 10:04”.
  • When they pay, the guest presses I’ve sent the payment. You get a text with one button — check Venmo, tap it, and the booking is final.
  • If the money never comes, the guest is reminded a day before the hold runs out, then the dates go back on sale by themselves and you’re told. Nothing sits half-done.
  • If nobody answers a request, you’re both texted again after 12 hours, and after a day it comes to Moritz too. A guest who waits a day usually books somewhere else.
  • The guest hears from you four more times, automatically: a week before, at 9am on the morning they arrive with the door code, Wi-Fi and parking, a short note on checkout morning, and a thank-you the day after.

One safety rule worth knowing: if it can’t send a complete arrival message — because the door code or Wi-Fi isn’t set — it sends nothing and tells you to do it yourself. It will never email a guest a half-finished set of instructions.

Worth knowing

The one thing it can’t do

Airbnb only re-reads our calendar every few hours — about three. So between you accepting a direct booking and Airbnb noticing, there’s a window where an Instant Book guest could still grab those nights. That’s exactly the problem you described, and no calendar link can close it.

There’s a free fix, and it’s yours to choose: turn Instant Book off on Airbnb. With Request to Book, nobody can take dates without you accepting, and the window stops mattering. The cost is that Airbnb favours Instant Book listings a little in search.

Separately, and free today: Airbnb has scheduled messages built into the host app. That gets your Airbnb guests the same day-of house information right now, without waiting for any of this. Most of your bookings are still there.

Your call, later

The other way, if you’d rather

There are booking systems that sit behind a site like yours and talk to Airbnb properly, rather than through a calendar link. The best fit is about $50–65 a month, no contract. Two things it does that we can’t:

  • It closes the window above completely. Airbnb asks it at the moment a guest taps Book, so a clash is refused outright.
  • It messages your Airbnb guests too, not just the ones who book direct.

The catch is real: it takes cards through Stripe, not Venmo. That’s about $29 on a $1,000 booking — set against the roughly $155 you save on that same booking by going direct.

What we’d do: start with what’s built. You haven’t taken a direct booking yet, and paying $600 a year and changing how guests pay before the first one is the wrong order. Nothing is wasted if you move later — the buttons and the emails stay exactly as they are.

Needs you

How long should dates stay held?

After you accept, the nights are off sale everywhere while the guest pays. If nothing lands by the deadline they go back on the calendar by themselves.

Needs you

Airbnb Instant Book: on or off?

See “the one thing it can’t do” above. Off is the free fix for the double-booking risk; on gets you a little more Airbnb traffic.

Needs you

Cards alongside Venmo?

Venmo works, but somebody has to look and confirm the money arrived. A card payment confirms the booking on its own, the second it goes through — at about 3% of the total.
