And the $500 sponsored article

Making it effortless to list.

This is where I think the real strategy is, and it comes straight from your own advantage. You have the relationships. So the product’s whole job is to turn a relationship into inventory with no more friction than sending a text.

Every marketplace dies of a supply problem, and the supply problem is almost always the form. A dealer with forty homes will not fill in forty forms. A private seller looking at a photo uploader closes the tab.

So: no form. A dealer sends whatever they already have, in whatever shape they already have it, and a listing appears.

  • Text or WhatsApp the photos inDealers already send inventory this way. No new habit to learn.
  • Forward an emailWhatever they already emailed someone else about the home.
  • Paste a linkTheir listing on another site, or a page on their own website.
  • Drop a spreadsheet, a PDF or a folder of photosForty homes in one go, in whatever shape they keep them.
  • Snap photos on a phoneFor the seller standing in the home right now. No account needed to start.

Behind all of those, the model reads the inputs and works out which value is the year, the make, the size, the price, the location — what each photo shows, whether the condition described matches them, whether two submissions are the same home. Then a listing page appears for them to correct rather than compose. Nothing is published until they say so.

The one that changes the conversation

Give the site one link and it keeps itself in sync. A dealer points it at their own inventory page, or their profile elsewhere. A new home appears there, it appears here. Sold there, it comes down here. Their ongoing effort is zero, because they already maintain that page for other reasons.

That turns your ask to a dealer from “please do some work for me” into “we will do the work for you, for free, permanently.” For someone deciding whether to bother with a new regional site, that is a completely different question. One caution: mirroring a dealer’s listings from someone else’s platform needs their written permission and still touches that platform’s rules — their own website or a file they send over is cleaner, and I would start there.

What comes back is a listing, not a form: photos straightened and ordered, a hero image chosen, the description in clean English. Better than what they would have made themselves, and fast enough to feel like a trick — so it is also a link they want to send to buyers, which is how the site spreads without anyone marketing it.

If their listing on your site is the best-looking version of their home anywhere, and it took ninety seconds, supply stops being the problem. That is the bet.

For trust
