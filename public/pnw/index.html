    PNW Mobile Homes — Long-Term Platform Vision

    The Vision
    Build the easiest place in America to find, compare, understand and purchase a manufactured home. PNW Mobile Homes begins with Washington, Oregon and Idaho as the proving ground, but the underlying technology should be designed from the beginning so the model can expand into additional U.S. regions and ultimately nationwide.

    What Makes It Different
    This is not intended to become another classifieds website. The platform should understand the manufactured-home market itself. A home may be a unique used/private-party listing, an individual dealer-stock home, or a manufacturer model offered by multiple dealers. The system should eventually allow a buyer to find a model and compare every participating dealer offering that same home—price, location, availability, options, delivery, setup and what's included.

    The Buyer Experience
    A buyer should eventually be able to say:
    "Show me 3+ bedroom, 2-bath manufactured homes under $180,000 within 200 miles of my ZIP code."
    Then compare homes side-by-side. Longer term, the platform should help answer the much bigger question:
    "Can I actually buy this home, put it where I want it, and what will the entire project cost?"
    That means connecting the home price with transportation, setup, foundation/site work, permits, utilities, financing information, parks/communities and the businesses needed to complete the project.

    Core Data Architecture
    The platform should be structured around connected entities rather than isolated listings:
    Manufacturer → Model → Dealer → Dealer Offer / Inventory
    alongside:
    Private/Used Homes · Parks & Communities · Transporters · Installers · Contractors · Other Industry Services
    A model should exist once in the database while multiple dealers can have different offers/inventory attached to it. This architecture is important for future comparison functionality.

    Inventory at Scale
    The long-term platform cannot depend on Ben or staff manually maintaining thousands of listings. Dealers should be able to manage their own inventory, while the system should support automated/authorized imports or syncing from dealer websites, feeds, spreadsheets and other sources. AI can assist with extracting listing information, standardizing data, matching models and identifying duplicates, with appropriate approval/moderation.

    Business Model
    Prioritize inventory and buyer traffic first rather than putting the marketplace behind listing fees. Monetization can develop around dealer/business subscriptions, featured placement, enhanced profiles, parks/communities, advertising/sponsorships, private-seller upgrades, qualified service leads and other marketplace services. The goal is recurring, scalable revenue rather than depending primarily on individual listing fees.

    Expansion Strategy
    Prove the system in WA / OR / ID, then expand into additional regions using the same underlying platform/database rather than building separate technology for every market. Regional brands or landing experiences can exist on top of one scalable national infrastructure.

    10-Year Goal
    Build a national manufactured-housing marketplace capable of generating multi-million-dollar annual revenue, with recurring revenue, substantial organic traffic, a proprietary manufacturer/model/dealer/inventory database and a business that can operate without the founder managing daily listings or transactions.

    The Architectural Principle
    Build PNW Mobile Homes today as the first region of the company we may want ten years from now—not as a regional classifieds site we'll have to rebuild later.

    The four irreversible choices it implies

    1. Manufacturer → Model → Dealer → Offer/Inventory. A model exists once; private/used homes sit alongside.
    2. One codebase, region as data. WA/OR/ID proves the system.
    3. Dealers own inventory. They control and approve it; you never maintain their listings.
    4. Monetise after inventory and traffic. Free listings now; revenue options follow usage.

    Making it effortless to list.

    This is where I think the real strategy is, and it comes straight from your own advantage. You have the relationships. So the product’s whole job is to turn a relationship into inventory with no more friction than sending a text.

    Every marketplace dies of a supply problem, and the supply problem is almost always the form. A dealer with forty homes will not fill in forty forms. A private seller looking at a photo uploader closes the tab.

    So: no form. A dealer sends whatever they already have, in whatever shape they already have it, and a listing appears.

    • Text or WhatsApp the photos inDealers already send inventory this way. No new habit to learn.
    • Forward an emailWhatever they already emailed someone else about the home.

    • Drop a spreadsheet, a PDF or a folder of photosForty homes in one go, in whatever shape they keep them.
    • Snap photos on a phoneFor the seller standing in the home right now. No account needed to start.

    Behind all of those, the model reads the inputs and works out which value is the year, the make, the size, the price, the location — what each photo shows, whether the condition described matches them, whether two submissions are the same home. Then a listing page appears for them to correct rather than compose. Nothing is published until they say so.
